From the interviews, this model emerged of how marketing managers create value with agentic AI. It links four steps: observing external conditions, navigating the organizational context, applying agentic AI, and obtaining value outcomes.

In practice, the same use case can create very different results depending on that internal context, which is why navigating the organization sits at the center of the model.

<ModelFigure />

## Key takeaways

- The manager's key concern should be creating the right organizational context to enable AI adoption and use.
- Access to usable data and tools for AI is especially critical, and often blocked by scarce technical resources or an overly rigid compliance framework.
- A compliant "laboratory" can give teams room to experiment without breaking policy.
- Marketing and technology leaders need to work closely so the right infrastructure and talent are available.

## Themes

A few themes kept returning across the conversations:

<Theme title="Marketing leader, or tech leader?">
Earlier research argued that technology leaders need to become business leaders. This study turns that around: marketing leaders increasingly need to become technology leaders too, taking on a strategic role and speaking the language of the technical teams whose data, tooling, and talent agentic AI depends on. As one CMO put it, she needs to be “the CTO's best friend.”
</Theme>

<Theme title="Agents might be the solution to their problems">
Agentic AI is often blamed for the very risks it creates: hallucinated answers, content that misses the brand. Yet several managers now point the same technology back at the problem: agents trained on brand guidelines that check content before it ships, or governance agents that score quality against a human baseline. The risk most associated with agentic AI turns out to be one it can also help solve.
</Theme>

<Theme title="Context beats use cases">
The identical use case produced opposite outcomes depending on the firm. Two managers connected self-service analytics directly to well-integrated data sources and had answers within minutes. A third, chasing the same goal, hit friction at data-ingestion and fell back to manual work. The technology was the same; the surrounding data infrastructure was not.
</Theme>

<Theme title="The seniority paradox">
One manager described agentic AI as giving him “whole armies of those super interns”; another went further: “I don't need two of those [senior] guys, I need one, a junior, and he can do the same thing.” Yet without seniors doing the work, who grows into the next generation of seniors? As one interviewee warned, this risks leaving organizations with no talent pipeline at all.
</Theme>
